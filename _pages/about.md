

I am an assistant professor in the College of Design and Engineering Studies (CoDES) at Wesleyan University. At Wesleyan, I lead the Wesleyan Integrated Microsystems Laboratory (WIMS).

My research and teaching explore the interplay of engineering, design, materials, physics, and chemistry that enables microelectromechanical systems (MEMS), which are ubiquitous in our world. My interests and experience from roles in industry and academia spans a range of applications including vaporizable microsystems, chemical sensors, micro-acoustic filters, tunable ferroelectric devices, and inertial sensors. Across this range of applications the manufacturing methods, analysis and design principles, and experimental test methodologies often invoke the same fundamental tools of design and engineering. At Wesleyan, my research projects aim to study the use of tunable materials for computation and security applications, nonlinear mechanical devices for multimodal sensors, and energy-delivery components for harsh environment sensor operation, by means of design and simulation tools, lab experiments for device testing and characterization, and data analysis and synthesis.
